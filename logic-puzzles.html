Logic puzzles: ordering clues

An ordering puzzle gives you short statements like "Ana is taller than Ben." You then answer a question such as "Who is the shortest?" You never have to guess. The clues always give enough information to build a full line.

Method: build a chain

  1. Rewrite every clue in the same direction. "Ben is shorter than Cleo" becomes "Cleo is taller than Ben."
  2. Look for a name that appears in two clues. That is where the clues connect.
  3. Link the clues into one line, tallest on the left and shortest on the right.
  4. Read the answer off the line.
Example. Clues: Dev is shorter than Ana. Eli is shorter than Dev. Fay is taller than Ana.
Rewrite: Ana is taller than Dev. Dev is taller than Eli. Fay is taller than Ana.
Chain: Fay, Ana, Dev, Eli. The second tallest is Ana. The shortest is Eli.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up directions. Always convert to one direction first.
  • Assuming a clue you were not given. If the puzzle does not say how two people compare directly, use the chain.
  • Stopping at the first answer that sounds right. Finish the whole line, then answer.

Why this type is good practice

Ordering puzzles train you to hold several facts in mind and combine them, which is the same skill used in scheduling, rankings and many everyday decisions. Writing the chain on paper is allowed and speeds you up.
