Odd one out: find the rule that breaks

In an odd one out puzzle, four of the five numbers share a property and one does not. You do not need to name the property in advance. Test a short list of ideas until four numbers agree.

Idea 1: multiples

Do four numbers divide evenly by the same small number? In 18, 27, 45, 63, 40 the first four are multiples of 9. 40 is not. Try 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 in that order.

Idea 2: primes

A prime has no divisors except 1 and itself. In 11, 17, 23, 29, 51 the first four are prime. 51 is 3 times 17, so it does not belong.

Idea 3: squares and powers of 2

Learn the squares up to 12 (1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144) and the powers of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256). Both lists appear a lot.

Idea 4: digit sums

Add the digits of each number. In 18, 27, 36, 45, 53 the digits of the first four add up to 9. 53 adds up to 8.

Idea 5: even, odd and palindromes

Check whether four are even and one is odd. In hard mode, also check for palindromes, numbers that read the same backwards, such as 121 or 44.

Fast habit: check even or odd first, then multiples, then squares or primes, then digit sums. That order solves almost every puzzle.
